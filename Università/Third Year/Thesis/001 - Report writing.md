# Feedback

I typically read your thesis and make comments and suggestions for improvements. Also, I might not be able to read/review last-minute drafts (I might be out etc). Thus, be sure that you send me content at least one-two weeks before the deadline.

I prefer to work on the PDF so I can write on it. Please avoid sending me 50 pages all at once, rather send me a couple of chapters at a time as soon as they are ready.

Before sending the thesis to me, make a pass with Grammarly or any other tool that checks for mistakes etc. Also, read it yourself a couple of times to make sure it makes sense and there is anything else you can improve (i.e., be sure that you do not see anything else that could be done better).

Be sure to implement the changes I suggest you to apply.

# Before writing

Before writing, prepare an outline (i.e., a bullet list where each point is a chapter, and each chapter is further divided in another bullet point list (one bullet per Section), and so on recursively). Let’s discuss the outline before you start writing.

As a target audience, consider a Computer Science professor, which however might not necessarily be familiar with HPC/GPUs/collectives/etc. So it is ok to introduce concepts as MPI, but no need to explain what a network interface is or what a network is.

There are no minimum/maximum length requirements. Still, bachelor thesis are typically 30-50 pages long (sometimes longer), whereas master thesis around 50-100 pages long. Length does not reflect quality, so there might be excellent 30 pages master thesis, as well as horrible 100 pages bachelor thesis. Do not fill the pressure to write a lot. Putting too much information is often not a good thing (e.g., do not explain how MPI collective work if you are not using collectives), just introduce the concepts that you need to explain what you did during your internship/thesis.

I have nothing against using LLMs, but please use it in a proper way. I.e., use them just for rephrasing/editing rather than for writing it from scratch. My experience is that text generated by LLMs (unless instructed otherwise) is horrible and sometimes wrong. It often uses too much bullet points, and explain/write things in a too schematic way, which is not how a human would write. Avoid bullet-points as much as you can (use them only when strictly necessary). Also, double (triple) check that there are no hallucinations, wrong/incorrect information, made-up references, etc…

The way I typically use them for writing is the following: I like a couple of paragraph on my own, putting together the ideas I want to express, maybe in a not so good English. Then I ask an LLM to rephrase/edit it in a more clear way (i.e., with a better use of English). Then I check twice it did not alter what I wanted to say.

Every Figure/Table/Code/etc that you put in the text should be referenced at least once in the text, and described/discussed.

Avoid having not numbered subsubsections (better to have an additional level of depth in the numbering, e.g. “x.y.z.w Results on LUMI”, rather than just “Results on LUMI”), otherwise the reader gets lost.

# Structure of the report

The report/paper will contain the following sections:
- Abstract section.
- Introduction/motivation section.
- Background/state of the art section.
- Design/implementation section.
- Evaluation section.
- Conclusions/future work section.
